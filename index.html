Key insights

  1. Two crops, 77% of the land. Rice (49%) and maize (28%) account for most Rabi area in these districts.
  2. Maize is the productivity story. It yields about 6.8 t/ha vs 3.4 for rice, so on just 57% of rice’s area it produces more tonnage (4.56M vs 4.01M tonnes).
  3. Crop diversity narrowed. Cereals rose from ~71% to ~90% of area while pulses fell from 9% to 5% and oilseeds from 13% to 3%. A candidate driver is expanding irrigation, a hypothesis worth testing with water-access data.
  4. Groundnut is quietly improving. Yield climbed from 1.8 to 3.1 t/ha (+75%) even as its area shrank, suggesting a shift to better practice rather than more land.
Analyst’s caution: Telangana re-drew its districts in 2016 (Bhadradri was carved from Khammam; Warangal was split). The sharp area drops after 2015 partly reflect new boundaries, so I compare shares and yields over time rather than raw area.

SQL showcase

Year-over-year area change per district using a CTE and the LAG window function. The full file has 7 queries (DENSE_RANK, conditional aggregation, self-join, audit).

WITH a AS (SELECT d.district, f.year_start, SUM(f.area_ha) area_ha FROM fact_crop_production f JOIN dim_district d USING(district_id) JOIN dim_crop c USING(crop_id) WHERE c.is_aggregate = 0 GROUP BY d.district, f.year_start) SELECT district, year_start, area_ha, ROUND(100.0*(area_ha - LAG(area_ha) OVER w) / LAG(area_ha) OVER w, 1) AS yoy_pct FROM a WINDOW w AS (PARTITION BY district ORDER BY year_start);
